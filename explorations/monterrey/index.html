    How the scores work

    Strava activity. Strava Global Heatmap walk and ride tiles (zoom 13, ~9 m pixels) are averaged into each hexagon and ranked as metro percentiles. Brighter heat means people are more comfortable there.

    Walk / bike to EGyTP. The cheapest route along the OpenStreetMap street network. Each segment costs its length × 1.0 on the busiest streets, 2.0 at the median, up to 5.0 on faint ones and 6.0 with no activity. Motorways are closed to walking. Motorways and trunk roads stay open only on stretches with at least median activity. Score 100 at the door and 50 at 4 km (walk) or 10 km (bike) of comfort-weighted distance.

    Everyday places. Real walking distance along the street network from the hexagon centre to the nearest place. Sources are DENUE (INEGI, records to 2026-04) and OpenStreetMap. Convenience store means abarrotes (SCIAN 461110) and minisupers (462112). Panadería means panificación tradicional (311812) plus shops named “panadería”. Pharmacy covers 464111 and 464112. Cinema covers 512130 plus OSM cinemas. Coffee shop means every café named for coffee in DENUE restaurant and café codes (7225xx), plus coffee chains by brand and OSM cafés. Google Places cafés are added to both coffee layers. Specialty coffee keeps independent places that Google returns for “café de especialidad” or “specialty coffee” and whose main type is a coffee shop, café or roastery. Chains (Starbucks, Tim Hortons, Caffenio, Punta del Cielo, Dunkin’ and others) are excluded. DENUE and OSM cafés that Google doesn’t list stay in with an average rating. Each café gets a Bayesian rating, (reviews × stars + 50 × metro mean) ÷ (reviews + 50), so a 4.9 from 8 reviews can’t beat a 4.8 from 1,000. Its quality weight runs from 0 at 4.4 to 1 at 4.75 and above, and cafés at 4.4 or below are dropped. The page shows only a coarse tier (top, upper, listed) per café, not its rating. A hexagon scores its best option, quality × walking-distance decay (50 at 1 km), so a top café 1 km away can beat an average one next door. Parks are OSM parks, public gardens and recreation grounds of at least 500 m², reached at any point on their edge. Score 50 at 400 m (convenience, park), 600 m (panadería, pharmacy, coffee shop) or 2 km (cinema).

    Routes and ranges. Every distance is walked along the street network from the hexagon’s access point, the street spot nearest its centre. Click a row in the card to see that route. The range under each place is the 10th–90th percentile of the same walk measured from every street point inside the hexagon.

    Colonias. INEGI doesn’t publish colonia boundaries, but every DENUE business lists its colonia. Each ~0.015 km² hexagon (H3 resolution 10) takes the colonia most of its businesses report, then gaps of up to ~500 m are filled from the neighbours. Boundaries are approximate, and areas with few businesses stay unlabelled.

    Leisure visitors. Anonymous smartphone location data for 2024. Each device’s stops are labelled home, work or other. We keep “other” stops during leisure hours (weekdays 18:00–22:00, weekends 07:00–23:00) and count only the minutes inside those windows. A stop counts fully for its own hexagon and fades with distance, weight e−d/150 m: about 0.13 for the next ring of hexagons and 0.02 two rings out, nothing beyond. Visitors who live within 700 m are left out, so the measure captures people who travel there. Two weighted counts per hexagon: visitor-hours, and the number of distinct home hexagons the visitors come from. Each is divided by its 99th percentile across the map and square-rooted (capped at 1), and the score is 100 × the geometric mean of the two. A high score needs both many hours and many origins, and only real hotspots get near 100. The card shows the plain counts within ~500 m. Devices skew toward smartphone owners and apps that share location, so read the score as relative.

    Safety (municipal). The share of violent crimes among all carpetas de investigación of the fuero común, 2023–2025, from SESNSP municipal open data. Violent means homicidio doloso, feminicidio, lesiones dolosas, secuestro, extorsión, violación and every robbery “con violencia”. The score is relative to the safest metro municipality with at least 1,000 carpetas (San Pedro, 7.2%): 100 × 7.2% ÷ the municipality’s share. Monterrey (12.5%) scores 58 and García (14.7%) 49. Nuevo León publishes no geolocated or colonia-level crime data, so every hexagon in a municipality gets the same value.

    Combined index. The weighted mean of the chosen scores, then multiplied by a safety factor. Safety never raises a hexagon’s index. The factor is 1 in the safest municipality and drops with the violent share: factor = 1 − (slider ÷ 5) × (1 − safety score ÷ 100). At full slider, García’s hexagons keep 49% of their index and Monterrey’s 58%; at the default (1) they keep 90% and 92%. Bivariate classes split each variable into thirds of the mapped area.

    Limits. Strava users skew sporty. DENUE locations can be a few metres off and may list closed businesses. Distances ignore slope, sidewalk quality and safety. Read it as a relative guide.
